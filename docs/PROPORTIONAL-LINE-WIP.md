# L29 proportional line control: source scope

The current source curriculum contains 29/60 authored draft trilingual lessons. L29 includes full theory, safety, worked examples, guided and independent activities, expected observations, three explained questions, troubleshooting, teacher adaptations and evidence rubric. Concept and arithmetic checks accompany a three-gain laboratory and runnable starter/reference programs. Published 0.8.1 artifacts remain unchanged at 23 lessons; this increment is unpackaged.

Error is right reflectance minus left reflectance. Correction is gain times error, clipped to ±30 before forming normalized commands 20 minus/plus correction, hence a wheel-command range of −10 to +50. The actual simulator uses binary reflectance, so the moving program applies this rule to −1/0/+1 errors. The helper accepts declared fractional arithmetic examples; plots do not invent continuous reflectance, physical noise or motor measurements. Both light is handled as latched line loss before issuing motion; manual stop takes priority and remains latched after release. The 6-second observation ends with an explicit stop and 300 ms settling wait.

Default gains 1/8/40 share the same initial offset, stripe/probe geometry, wheel acceleration and 20 ms updates. They first reach both-dark readings at 900/240/120 ms. Their actual RMS lateral body errors are approximately 0.006079/0.005135/0.004934 m. The high-gain trial clips 30 updates. All three have one nonzero correction-sign reversal; these fixtures do not demonstrate sustained oscillation. The lesson uses actual sluggish-response and saturation evidence rather than labeling every high-gain trial unstable. All default trials complete without collision, but do not reach the distant course goal.

The lab requires three distinct gains, rejects blanks and out-of-range values, invalidates edited comparisons and exports all three full traces with a learner explanation of at least 10 characters. The graph shows actual lateral error over time; the table retains first both-dark time, RMS error, reversals and clipped samples. A first both-dark reading is not a claim of zero body displacement. The zero-gain alternative preserves nonresponse instead of fabricating a convergence time.
